23       Summary and Analysis of Proposition 132
24       What is fentanyl?
25       The term “fentanyl” refers to a type of potent synthetic opioid. Fentanyl is used medically for
26       pain relief, but possessing or distributing fentanyl without a prescription is illegal. Fentanyl has
27       become the leading cause of drug overdose deaths in both Colorado and the United States
28       because it can be fatal in very small amounts and is often mixed into other illicit drugs without a
29       user’s knowledge. Fentanyl belongs to a class of potent synthetic opioids with similar
30       characteristics. The measure impacts fentanyl along with these related drugs.
31       What are the current penalties for fentanyl offenses in Colorado?
32       Penalties for fentanyl offenses are based on the type and quantity of the drug, and whether the
33       offense involved personal use or intent to distribute. Current penalties range from court-ordered
34       treatment, fines, and probation to time in prison. State law specifies which fentanyl offenses
35       require prison sentences, which qualify for alternative penalties and reduced charges, or are
36       subject to judicial discretion. The severity of the penalty also depends on the weight of the

 2   rather than the amount of fentanyl itself.
 3   Who can and cannot go to prison under current law?
 4   The most serious fentanyl-related drug crimes mandate a minimum prison sentence of 8 years
 5   up to a maximum of 32 years. This includes offenders who:
 6   ●   distributed to a minor;
 7   ●   distributed more than 50 grams of a substance; or
 8   ●   distributed at least 4 grams of a substance that caused death.

 9   In other cases, state law allows for sentences such as probation and treatment, and prohibits
10   state prison for offenders who either:
11   ●   possessed less than 1 gram of a substance with no intent to distribute and do not have
12       multiple past fentanyl convictions;
13   ●   possessed between 1 to 4 grams of a substance with no intent to distribute and provided
14       evidence they were unaware the substance contained fentanyl; or
15   ●   distributed less than 4 grams and were eligible for “Good Samaritan” protections that reduce
16       criminal penalties for reporting an overdose and staying until help arrives.
17   When prison time is neither mandated nor prohibited by state law, a judge may determine a
18   sentence within a penalty range. In these scenarios, the judge may consider the unique
19   circumstances of the case, including if the offender was aware of fentanyl in the substance,
20   criminal history, risk to public safety, acceptance of responsibility, or any other circumstance that
21   is relevant to consider at sentencing. Judges may also reduce or modify a penalty in exchange
22   for successful completion of treatment.
23   How will penalties change under Proposition 132?
24   Proposition 132 increases criminal penalties for fentanyl offenses by expanding felony charges
25   for possession and distribution, imposing longer prison sentences, and limiting judicial
26   discretion.
27   For those convicted of distributing any amount of fentanyl, the measure requires a prison
28   sentence of between 8 and 32 years. It eliminates the possibility for reduced penalties in “Good
29   Samaritan” cases involving distribution.
30   The measure also makes possession of any amount of fentanyl a felony. However, if a person
31   possesses less than one gram and successfully completes court-ordered drug rehabilitation
32   treatment, the felony charge may be reduced to a misdemeanor. Individuals charged with a
33   felony for possessing more than one gram are no longer eligible for this reduction, but the
34   measure keeps "Good Samaritan" penalty reductions for other low-level possession offenses.
35   Although not mandated, the measure permits varying lengths of imprisonment for possession.
36   Judicial discretion is limited by imposing mandatory minimum prison sentences for fentanyl
37   distribution cases. Additionally, courts must order treatment for individuals charged with
38   possessing less than one gram of a substance containing fentanyl, regardless of the

 6   Argument For Proposition 132
 7   1) Fentanyl is highly potent and deadly, and it warrants stronger penalties than other drugs.
 8      The measure targets drug traffickers with harsher sentences and disrupts the distribution of
 9      fentanyl, including when it is mixed into other substances. Mandated prison sentences for
10      drug dealers are likely to enhance public safety by keeping them off the street, as well as
11      provide victims and their families with a greater sense of justice. The measure also increases
12      accountability for drug users and may deter fentanyl use, which could help reduce overdose
13      deaths. Proposition 132 demonstrates Colorado’s commitment to addressing fentanyl-
14      related crime.


15   Argument Against Proposition 132
16   1) Proposition 132 is unlikely to reduce fentanyl use, lower crime rates, or prevent overdose
17      deaths. Individuals may be discouraged from seeking addiction treatment, or calling for
18      emergency assistance during overdoses, due to fear of criminal prosecution and prison time.
19      The measure treats people who distribute trace amounts of fentanyl as major drug dealers.
20      In addition, longer prison sentences are expensive and would shift resources away from

 6   Fiscal Impact of Proposition 132
 7   State Revenue
 8   Proposition 132 increases state revenue to the Judicial Department by $23,000 in the first year,
 9   and $73,000 in the second and future years from additional criminal fines due to the measure’s
10   increase in crime classification.
11   State Spending
12   Proposition 132 increases state spending to cover increased criminal justice system costs for the
13   courts, public defenders, and prisons. These costs are paid from the state General Fund.
14   Specifically, the measure increases state spending in the first budget year by $0.9 million to pay
15   for costs in the courts and for public defenders. In the second year, state spending under the
16   measure is estimated to increase to $8.9 million as more individuals are sent to prison for
17   distributing or possessing. Prison costs will continue to increase as individuals are incarcerated
18   for longer periods of time. By year five, total state spending is estimated to increase by $68.2
19   million. In addition, due to the estimated increase in the future prison population, the state may
20   need to construct or contract for additional prison space.
21   The measure does not specify a funding source to pay for this increased criminal justice-related
22   spending, and it is assumed that these costs will be paid from the General Fund. While the
23   measure may reduce money available for other uses (the three largest of which are Health Care
24   Policy and Financing, Education, and Higher Education), the effect of the measure on other
25   programs is unknown and will depend on future budgeting decisions by the state legislature.
26   Local Government
27   Similar to the state, costs will increase for county-funded district attorneys to prosecute longer
28   cases with enhanced penalties.
